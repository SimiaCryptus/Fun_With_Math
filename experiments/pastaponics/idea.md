## **Programmable Tubular Rhizosphere Substrate (PTRS)**

*A modular, 3D‑printed hydroponic medium enabling plant‑driven nutrient self‑regulation through spatially differentiated nutrient channels.*

## **1\. Project Overview**

The PTRS system is a **synthetic proto‑soil** composed of a lattice of porous 3D‑printed tubes. Each tube acts as:

* a **structural scaffold** for root anchoring
* a **nutrient delivery conduit**
* a **programmable microenvironment**
* a **biome colonization site**

By feeding **different nutrient solutions into different tubes**, the plant can **self‑regulate** nutrient uptake by selectively colonizing preferred channels. This recreates the evolutionary conditions of early root development (Carboniferous lignin‑substrate era) but with modern control.

## **2\. Core Scientific Hypothesis**

> **Plants will differentially colonize nutrient‑rich tubes, enabling autonomous nutrient regulation and adaptive root architecture.**

This relies on known root behaviors:

* gradient sensing
* selective foraging
* transporter modulation
* microbe recruitment
* architectural plasticity

The PTRS amplifies these behaviors by providing **high‑contrast, spatially distinct nutrient zones**.

## **3\. System Architecture**

### **3.1 Tubular Lattice Geometry**

A 3D‑printed structure composed of:

* interconnected tubes (2–10 mm diameter)
* variable pore sizes (50–500 µm)
* multi‑layered lattice (stacked or radial)
* modular cartridges for easy replacement

Geometry goals:

* maximize root exploration
* create predictable nutrient gradients
* allow biome colonization
* maintain structural rigidity

### **3.2 Tube Functional Types**

Each tube can be assigned a role:

1. **N‑Channel** (nitrogen-rich)
2. **P‑Channel** (phosphorus-rich)
3. **K‑Channel** (potassium-rich)
4. **Micro‑Channel** (micronutrients)
5. **Bio‑Channel** (microbial inoculation)
6. **Hormone‑Channel** (auxins, cytokinins, etc.)
7. **Stress‑Channel** (salt, drought mimics for research)
8. **Water‑Channel** (pure hydration)

This creates a **programmable nutrient landscape**.

### **3.3 Pore Distribution**

Pores are engineered to:

* allow nutrient diffusion
* permit microbial colonization
* encourage root wrapping
* maintain structural integrity

Pore density can be tuned per tube type.

### **3.4 Materials**

Candidate materials:

* PLA or PETG (biologically inert)
* flexible TPU for root‑friendly surfaces
* ceramic composites for porosity
* bioresins for microbial compatibility

Material must be:

* non‑toxic
* porous or micro‑perforated
* structurally stable
* sterilizable
