# Squirrely

## What is this?

Squirrely is a browser-based arcade game about a squirrel trying to survive a highway crossing. You're not just dodging generic traffic — every car on the road is a "driver" with its own personality and behavior. Your job is simple to state and hard to master: get acorns off the road and stash them safely in one of four tree hollows on the far side, without getting flattened.

## The idea behind it

Classic road-crossing games (think Frogger) treat traffic as a rhythm puzzle — cars move in predictable lanes and you time your hops. Squirrely keeps that hopping, timing-based core, but adds a layer of "reading" the road. Instead of anonymous obstacles, each vehicle color represents a distinct temperament:

- **Red** actively hunts you down.
- **Yellow** panics and drives erratically.
- **Blue** swerves out of its way to avoid you.
- **Green** gets aggressive and rages when traffic jams up.
- **Purple** simply doesn't notice you at all.
- **White** just drives steady, no surprises.

This turns the traffic into something you have to psychologically anticipate rather than purely mechanically time. You start reading the road the way you might read personalities in real traffic — "that one's going to swerve at me," "that one won't even see me coming."

On top of survival, there's a light risk/reward foraging loop: acorns are scattered on the road, you can carry up to two in your mouth at once, and you need to ferry them safely to tree hollows to fill them all and complete the level. Smoke bombs are mixed in among the acorns — you can't bank those for points, but you can drop them as a roadside distraction to throw off pursuing traffic, adding a small tactical option to an otherwise fast, reflexive game.

## The controls and feel

Movement is grid-based hopping (arrow keys or WASD), with a dash move that covers two rows at once for emergency escapes, and a crouch to stay low and cautious. There's even a taunt button, for players who like living dangerously or just enjoy the flavor. On touchscreens, the same vocabulary of moves maps to swipes, taps, and holds, so it plays naturally on mobile as well as desktop.

The whole experience is rendered in a lightweight 3D style, giving the road, trees, and vehicles some depth and dimension beyond a flat top-down grid, while keeping the core gameplay simple enough to pick up in seconds.

## Why it's interesting

Squirrely is a good example of how a small change to a well-worn genre — giving obstacles "personality" instead of pure predictability — can meaningfully change how a game feels to play. It rewards pattern recognition and quick decision-making, but the patterns are dressed up as behavior rather than raw math, which makes the game feel more alive and a bit more mischievous.

It's also a nice showcase of squeezing a lot of expressiveness (six distinct traffic behaviors, a carrying/banking mechanic, environmental tools like smoke bombs, multi-input support) out of a compact, browser-playable arcade game — no installs, no accounts, just open a page and start dodging traffic.

## Who might enjoy this

- Fans of classic arcade road-crossing games looking for a modern twist.
- Casual players who want a quick, replayable browser game with some depth to master.
- Anyone curious about how simple "personality" rules can make simple AI feel more dynamic and readable.
- People who enjoy speedrunning or optimizing risk/reward loops — deciding when to grab a second acorn, when to dash, and when to just get out of the road alive.
