# Tomb Crawler

## What is this?

Tomb Crawler is a browser game about exploring an endless, procedurally
generated tomb built from hexagonal tiles. You guide a small explorer through
sandstone corridors and carved chambers, sweeping up gold coins as you go,
while keeping a wary eye out for the creatures that guard the ruins. When
things get dangerous, you have a limited supply of explosives to blast a path
through cracked walls, clear out enemies, or seal off a corridor behind you.

There's no fixed level to finish and no wall of doom chasing you from behind.
Instead, the game quietly raises the stakes the further you wander from where
you started: the tomb gets denser with guardians, coins become harder to find
nearby, and every decision to press onward or use one of your precious
explosives matters a little more.

## The idea behind it

Tomb Crawler borrows its heartbeat from the classic "collect everything on the
maze while something wanders around trying to stop you" style of arcade game
that's been a mainstay of the genre for decades. But rather than copy that
game's exact look and rules, it reinterprets the idea through a different
lens:

- The maze is **hexagonal** rather than a square grid, so every junction offers
  six directions of travel instead of four — it feels a bit more organic, and
  a bit less predictable.
- The world is **infinite**. It's generated on the fly, chunk by chunk, as you
  explore outward, so there's always more tomb ahead of you.
- Coins are **gone for good** once collected — there's no refill, no reset.
  This means the game naturally pushes you to keep moving into new, less
  familiar (and more dangerous) territory rather than farming a safe corner.
- Instead of one generic power-up, there's a small toolkit of **explosives**
  — plain bombs, incendiaries, and directional shaped charges — each with its
  own tactical use, its own risks, and its own trade-offs. Explosions clear
  enemies and open new paths, but they also destroy any coins caught in the
  blast, and can hurt you too if you're not careful about when and where you
  place them.
- The tomb's guardians range from harmlessly wandering beetles to statues that
  patrol fixed routes, serpents that lunge in straight lines, and jackal-headed
  hunters that actively give chase — giving the world a sense of escalating,
  place-based danger rather than a single repeating threat.

The visual theme is a sun-bleached, torch-lit ruin: stone corridors, cracked
walls waiting to be blown open, scarabs skittering in the dark, and gold
scattered through the wreckage. It deliberately avoids the iconography of the
genre it's inspired by, aiming instead for something that feels like its own
small world.

## What it looks like to play

You control your explorer with the keyboard (a hex-friendly cluster of keys,
or the arrow keys) or with swipes on a touch screen, picking a direction and
letting your character glide from cell to cell until a wall or a decision
point gets in the way. A heads-up display in the corner of the screen tracks
your score, remaining lives, how far you've ventured from the start, and
which explosive you currently have selected and ready to use. Runs typically
last somewhere between two and ten minutes, ending when your last life is
lost — at which point your score, how deep you got, and a few other stats are
tallied up, with your best runs remembered for next time.

## Why it might be interesting

- **If you enjoy classic arcade-style "collect and evade" games** but want a
  twist on the familiar formula, Tomb Crawler offers a fresh grid shape,
  a permanent sense of progress (nothing respawns), and meaningful
  decision-making around a toolkit of explosives instead of a single
  power-up.
- **If you're curious about procedural generation**, this is a good example
  of a world built from small, deterministic, seeded pieces that tile
  together seamlessly and can be generated indefinitely without ever needing
  to be authored by hand.
- **If you like games that reward exploration and calculated risk**, the
  "coins never come back" rule and the tension of choosing when to detonate
  a bomb create a steady stream of small, meaningful decisions rather than
  pure reflex-testing.
- **If you just want a quick, replayable arcade diversion** that runs
  straight in a web browser with no install, this fits a short coffee-break
  session as well as a longer, score-chasing marathon.

It's aimed at casual and arcade-game enthusiasts alike, players who liked the
genre's classic feel but want something visually and mechanically distinct,
and anyone who enjoys tinkering with "just one more run" style scoring and
high-score chasing.
